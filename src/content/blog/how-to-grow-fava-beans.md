Most of the vegetable garden is closing down now, which is exactly when fava beans (broad beans, if you learned to garden in Britain) can go in. Sown in fall where winters are mild, they sit through the cold as short sturdy plants and give you one of the first real harvests of the next year.

Where winters are hard, they go in at the other end, as early in spring as you can work the soil. Either way they want cool weather. Favas stop setting pods once summer heat arrives, so the whole point is to get them done before it does.

## Fall or spring

Fall sowing works where the ground doesn't freeze hard for long stretches: much of the UK, the Pacific Northwest, and the milder South. Pick a variety bred for it. 'Aquadulce Claudia' is the classic, and it's the one I'd start with. Sow a few weeks before your first frosts so the plants are a few inches tall going into winter, and they'll put on growth again the moment spring warms up.

Everywhere else, sow as soon as the soil can be worked in late winter or early spring. Fava seeds germinate in cold soil that would rot most beans, and young plants shrug off light frosts.

There's a risk to fall sowing that nobody puts on the packet: mice. A row of big beans just under the soil is a winter larder. If you've had trouble before, start them in deep pots in a cold frame and plant out the young plants instead.

## Sowing and spacing

Push each bean about two inches deep. The common layout is a double row, with the beans staggered about eight inches apart each way, and a wider path between pairs of rows. They're big plants by late spring and the air moving between them helps keep chocolate spot and other leaf diseases down.

Over winter, a sheet of fleece over the worst nights is all they usually need. Take it off again when the weather eases so the plants don't get soft.

## Support and pinching

Favas grow tall and top-heavy, and a spring gale will flatten a block of them. Knock in a post at each corner and run string around the whole block at a couple of heights as they grow. It's not elegant, but it holds them up.

The tips are where blackfly gather. Once the lowest flowers have set small pods, pinch out the top few inches of each plant. It takes away the soft growth the aphids like, and the plant puts its effort into the pods it already has. The tips are good to eat, cooked like spinach.

## When to pick

Start picking when you can see the beans as bumps along the pod and the pod still feels firm. Very young pods can be cooked whole. Left too long, the beans turn starchy and grow a thick grey skin you'll want to slip off after cooking.

One thing worth knowing: a small number of people, mostly with a condition called G6PD deficiency, react badly to fava beans. If that's in your family, check before you grow a crop of them.

**Pro tip.** When the crop is finished, cut the plants off at the soil and leave the roots in the bed. Like peas, favas carry nitrogen-fixing nodules on their roots, and they break down where they are.
