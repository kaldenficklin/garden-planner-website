A sunny windowsill feels like plenty of light in October. By late winter, when the first seeds go in, the days are short and the sun is low, and seedlings on the sill lean so hard toward the glass they nearly fall over.

A grow light fixes that, and it doesn't have to be expensive or complicated. Now is a good time to set one up, while there's nothing on the shelf and you can fiddle with it.

## The light itself

You don't need a specialist grow light with pink and purple diodes. Ordinary white LED shop lights, the long kind sold for garages and workshops, grow good seedlings. Look for ones described as full spectrum or daylight. Two of them side by side over a shelf cover a couple of standard seed trays.

The bigger, brighter LED panels sold for growing work too, but they're more than seedlings need and they're hung much higher. If you buy one of those, go by the maker's height guide rather than anything you read about shop lights.

## Distance is what people get wrong

Light falls off quickly with distance, and a shop light that looks bright from across the room is weak a foot and a half below. For ordinary LED shop lights, hang them a few inches above the tops of the seedlings. It looks too close. It isn't, because those lights run cool enough that the leaves won't scorch.

That's why the lights need to hang on chains, hooks or adjustable cords. Seedlings grow fast, and you'll raise the light every few days to keep that same small gap. Some people raise the trays on upturned boxes instead and take boxes out as the plants get taller. Either works.

The seedlings tell you if you've got it wrong. Long, pale, floppy stems mean the light is too far away. Lower it.

## Hours, timers and air

Run the lights 14 to 16 hours a day. More is not better: seedlings need a stretch of darkness each night too. A plug-in timer takes the job out of your hands, and you will forget otherwise. I know I did, for a whole weekend once.

A small fan on its lowest setting, pointed near the trays for part of the day, keeps air moving. Seedlings that sway a little grow thicker stems, and moving air helps keep the surface of the compost from staying damp enough for mold.

## Why set it up now

The first seeds of the year go in earlier than most people expect. Onions and leeks are usually started indoors around ten to twelve weeks before your last frost, which for a lot of gardens lands in January or February. If the lights are hung, plugged in and tested before then, sowing day is just sowing day.

**Pro tip.** Water and electricity share a shelf here. Plug the lights and timer into a GFCI outlet, keep plugs above where water could drip, and let each cord hang in a loop below the socket so drips fall off before they reach it.
