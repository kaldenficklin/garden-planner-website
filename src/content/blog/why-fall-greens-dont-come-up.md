Almost every fall greens failure I have had happened in the first week, before
there was anything to look at. You rake the bed, sow the row, come back nine days
later, and there is nothing. Not weak seedlings. Nothing.

That is a germination problem, and in August it is nearly always heat plus a
seedbed that dried out while you were at work. Lettuce in particular will simply
sit there when the soil is too warm, waiting for conditions it likes, and by the
time it gets them the season is short. The fix is unglamorous: sow in the
evening, keep the top half-inch damp twice a day, and put something over the bed
to knock the sun down until you see green.

The one that stings most is the last row on the chart. Waiting for comfortable
weather feels sensible and costs you the crop, because the days-to-maturity math
runs against your first frost, not against how pleasant it is to kneel in the
dirt.
