Nobody tells you that the difference between a good green bean and a bad one is
about four days. The plant does not signal it. The pod just quietly goes from
snapping cleanly to bending like a piece of string, and you find out at dinner.

August is when this gets expensive, because everything is producing at once and
you cannot get to all of it. So the useful thing is not a schedule, it is a cue
you can check in ten seconds walking down the row: the width of a bean, the
shape of a spinach leaf, whether there is a hard stem coming up through the
middle of an onion.

Beans and radishes are the two most people lose. Both reward picking earlier
than feels right, and both punish a week of inattention more than any other crop
on this chart.
