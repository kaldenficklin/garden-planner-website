Paperwhites are the bulb for anyone who missed the fall planting window, or who wants something white and green on the table in December without planning a year ahead. They need no cold treatment at all. You sit them on pebbles, add water, and in about four to six weeks the bowl is in flower.

Two things catch people out. The stems flop, and the smell is a lot stronger than the delicate little flowers suggest.

## Pebbles or soil, and where the water goes

Any bowl without a drainage hole works for the pebble method. Put down a few inches of gravel, pebbles or glass beads, set the bulbs on top with the pointed end up, close enough that they nearly touch, then tuck more pebbles around them until roughly the top half of each bulb shows. The pebbles are there to hold the bulbs steady once the stems get top-heavy.

Then add water until it just reaches the base of the bulbs. Just touching, or a little below. The roots grow down to find it, but a bulb sitting in water rots, and a rotting paperwhite smells worse than a blooming one. Check the level every couple of days, because a bowl full of roots drinks more than you'd think.

Potting mix works too, if you'd rather. Use a pot with a drainage hole, plant with the noses showing, and keep the mix moist without letting it go soggy. A pot of damp soil is heavier than a bowl of pebbles, which helps when the flowers open and everything wants to tip.

## Why they flop, and what helps

Paperwhites in a warm living room shoot up fast and tall, and somewhere around the time the buds open the whole clump leans over the rim. Warmth and poor light are the cause. A cool room with good light, or a bright windowsill well away from the radiator, grows shorter, sturdier stems.

Support is the easy fix. Push a ring of thin bamboo canes or twiggy prunings into the pebbles and run a loop of twine or raffia around the stems. It holds them up and looks deliberate. I do this on planting day now, because I never remember until the morning they've already fallen over.

There is also an alcohol trick, which is optional and needs care. The Flower Bulb Research Program at Cornell University found that a dilute alcohol solution keeps the stems shorter without harming the flowers. Start the bulbs in plain water. Once the shoots are an inch or two tall, pour that off and use roughly one part 40 percent spirit, such as vodka or gin, to seven parts water from then on. Stronger than that damages the plants, and wine or beer are no good because of the sugar. If any of it makes you uneasy, the canes do the job.

## Staggering, so the flowers last through December

A bowl stays in flower for a week or two, longer in a cool room, and then it's over. For flowers right through the holidays, buy all the bulbs at once and start a few every couple of weeks.

Keep the waiting bulbs somewhere cool, dry and dark, in a paper bag rather than plastic. They'll hold for several weeks like that, though eventually they start sprouting in the bag, which is your cue to plant them. A bowl started now, at the end of November, should flower around Christmas or just after, depending on how warm your house runs.

## Pets, and that scent

Paperwhites are narcissus, and narcissus is toxic to cats and dogs, the bulb most of all. A dog that digs one out of a bowl, or a cat that chews the leaves, is a reason to call your vet. Put the bowl where they can't reach it, and don't leave spare bulbs in an open bag on the floor.

The scent divides people. Some love it. Plenty of others think a full bowl in a small room smells like something has gone off. If you don't know which camp you're in, try one bowl in a big room before you plant a dozen. Some varieties are sold as milder-scented, so read the label if that matters to you.

When the flowers fade, most people put the bulbs on the compost. Bulbs forced in water have used up what they had, and paperwhites won't survive the winter outdoors where it freezes hard.

**Pro tip.** Grow them cool. A bright room on the chilly side gives shorter, sturdier stems than a warm living room, and the flowers last longer too.
