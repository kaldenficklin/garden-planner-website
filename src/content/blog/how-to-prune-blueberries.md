Blueberry pruning is mostly about taking out old wood so new wood can replace it. A bush that's never pruned keeps cropping, but the berries get smaller and the middle turns into a thicket of tired grey canes.

This is for highbush blueberries, the kind most of us grow, and it's a job for late winter. Written now so it's ready when the time comes.

## Why late winter

A dormant bush has dropped its leaves, so you can see every cane and how they sit. The buds are still tight, which means you can tell flower buds from leaf buds before you cut.

Late winter rather than early also means any cold damage has shown itself. A tip that died back in a hard freeze looks dry and dark by then, and you can take it off at the same time. Garden Planner Pro can hold a reminder for it, which is how I stopped finding myself out there in April with the bush already in flower.

## Old canes out, young canes in

Blueberries fruit on last year's growth, and the canes that carry the best crops are young to middle-aged. Once a cane gets to roughly six years old, it carries less and less. You'll recognise the old ones: thick, grey, rough-barked, with only short twiggy growth at the top.

On a mature bush, take one or two of the oldest canes out each year, cut right down at the base. New canes come up from the crown to replace them. The aim is a bush with canes of every age, so there's always something coming along.

Don't take out all the old wood in one go. It's easy to get carried away with a bush that's been neglected for years. Spread the renovation over two or three winters instead.

## Dead wood, low branches, and the middle

Before any of that, cut out anything dead, broken or obviously diseased. Then take off branches that sprawl along the ground, since fruit on them ends up dirty or eaten.

Next look into the centre. If shoots are crowding and rubbing, thin them so light and air get in. Berries in a shaded tangle ripen slowly and are harder to pick.

Last, look at the tips before you snip. Fat round buds near the ends of last year's shoots are flower buds, and each one is a cluster of berries. Thin pointed buds further down are leaf buds. Clipping every tip back to tidy the shape cuts off the crop.

## Young bushes, and the hard bit

For the first two or three years after planting, a blueberry needs almost no pruning. Take out anything dead or damaged and leave the rest to grow.

The harder advice is to pull off the flowers in the first year or two, so the plant puts its effort into roots and new canes. It's painful to do. I've skipped it more than once, and those bushes were noticeably slower to fill out than the ones I was strict with.

If you can't bring yourself to strip the lot, leave a few clusters on, just enough to taste.

**Pro tip.** Look at the tips before you cut. Fat round buds are this summer's flowers, so every one you remove is a cluster of berries. Thin pointed buds only make leaves.
